India: the world’s largest democracy. But who gets to vote?

A vote is one of the most fundamental expressions of an ordinary citizen’s voice and agency in a democracy. To take that right away is to strip people of their power to shape decisions that govern their lives.

Part 1: The voter rolls

01

What is the SIR?

Special Intensive Revision (SIR) is an exercise conducted by the Election Commission of India to revise electoral rolls. Its “stated” purpose is to identify and remove duplicate, relocated and otherwise ineligible voter entries. However, there has been no clarity or transparency as to the process behind voter removal from lists.

The central concern is whether eligible citizens are being excluded to favour the ruling party and whether they have a fair, accessible opportunity to restore their names and their rights.

02

Over 130 million voter entries excluded

13 crore (130 million) voter entries have been reportedly excluded from India’s draft electoral rolls under SIR.

Behind every entry is a question of identity, eligibility and representation. How many exclusions were justified? How many eligible citizens were wrongfully removed? And how many will be able to reclaim their right to vote before it is too late?

An electoral roll is not merely a list of names. It determines whose voice can be heard in a democracy.

03

Two out of three Election Commissioners raised objections

According to an investigation by The Indian Express, two of India’s three Election Commissioners recorded objections at least 14 times over 10 months.

Under Section 18 of the 2023 law, Election Commission decisions should be unanimous wherever possible. When commissioners disagree, decisions must be made by majority, not by the Chief Election Commissioner alone.

If two of the three Election Commissioners repeatedly raised objections, how were the disputed decisions reached and why does the Commission maintain they were unanimous?

04

The Supreme Court: “We did not approve it.”

On October 5, 2026, India’s Supreme Court clarified that it had not approved the disputed changes to Form 6, the application used to register new voters.

The Court sought records to establish who authorised the additional declaration introduced into the voter-registration process.

If the Supreme Court never approved these changes, who authorised them and under what legal authority?

Democracy is not just about holding elections. It is about ensuring that every eligible citizen has a voice in them.

Part 2

Delhi Police brutality: illegal, inhumane detentions

05

Over 6,000 detained. Delhi locked down.

On October 10, 2026, 6,000+ people were reportedly unlawfully detained amid protests against the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls.

Minors, students, protest organisers, activists, journalists, lawyers and leaders of the opposition parties have been attacked, crammed in caged buses, and sexually assaulted by the Delhi Police. Around 25,000 security force personnel were deployed, the internet was suspended in parts of Delhi, and roads and metro services were blocked.

Citizens challenging the electoral process faced detention, surveillance, and sweeping restrictions on their freedom of movement and assembly. The UN Human Rights Office expressed alarm over the arbitrary detentions and called for the immediate, unconditional release of everyone detained.

06

State and police violence: who is accountable?

Video footage and eyewitness accounts have documented police violently removing demonstrators, alongside assaults on protesters. Women protestors and journalists have also raised concerns of physical mistreatment and sexual harassment by officers during protests, while Muslim protesters and members of other minorities have reported being subjected to disproportionate violence.

Delhi Police falls under the Union Ministry of Home Affairs, headed by Amit Shah, within Prime Minister Narendra Modi’s government.

The responsibility for explaining these operations cannot stop with officers on the streets.

Who authorised the crackdown? Who supervised it? And who will be held accountable for the treatment of protesters?

07

Right-wing attacks as police stood by

Video footage and eyewitness reporting documented right-wing groups assaulting protesters in Delhi, including attacks carried out in front of police officers. Men identifying themselves with Bajrang Dal and RSS were reported attacking demonstrators and independent journalists.

While peaceful protesters were forcibly detained under restrictions on public gatherings, right-wing groups were able to assemble, threaten and attack demonstrators. The contrasting police response raises serious questions about selective enforcement and the failure to protect protesters from violence.

The violence is documented. The attackers’ organisational affiliations and the police response demand independent investigation.

08

What protesters are demanding

  1. Immediate release of all arbitrarily detained protesters, activists and journalists. End the crackdown on peaceful dissent.
  2. Resignation of Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar, an independent investigation into his conduct, and prosecution and appropriate punishment wherever criminal wrongdoing is established.
  3. Immediate suspension of SIR, independent scrutiny of electoral-roll exclusions, and restoration of eligible voters wrongfully removed.
  4. Accountability from the BJP-led Union government. Prime Minister Narendra Modi and Home Minister Amit Shah must answer for the crackdown, police violence and decisions taken under their government’s authority.
  5. Repeal the 2023 Election Commissioners Appointment Law and establish a genuinely independent, transparent appointment process.

What good is the “mother” of democracy if her people cannot vote?
